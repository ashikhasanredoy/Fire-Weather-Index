Parameter Guide & Explanations
Comprehensive breakdown of all 9 meteorological input features
Atmospheric Parameters
Temperature Temperature
Unit: °C Higher = Increased Risk
The ambient surface air temperature measured in degrees Celsius. Elevated temperatures accelerate the evaporation of moisture from vegetation and surface litter, substantially increasing ignition probability and fire spread velocity.
Typical Range: 22°C – 42°C Key Threshold: > 30°C promotes high fire vulnerability
Relative Humidity RH
Unit: % Lower = Increased Risk
The percentage of moisture saturation in the air. Low relative humidity drastically draws moisture out of dry grasses and leaves. When RH drops below 30%, forest fuels become exceptionally flammable.
Typical Range: 21% – 90% Critical Zone: < 30% indicates severe drying
Wind Speed Ws
Unit: km/h Higher = Increased Risk
The horizontal velocity of surface wind in km/h. Wind supplies abundant fresh oxygen to combustion zones and tilts the flames forward, pre-heating unburned vegetation downwind and accelerating fire front advance.
Typical Range: 6 – 29 km/h Effect: Drives the Initial Spread Index (ISI)
Rainfall Rain
Unit: mm (24h) Higher = Suppresses Risk
Total precipitation accumulated over the preceding 24-hour period. Rainfall directly re-saturates surface fuels and duff layers, reducing ignition potential and dampening fire behavior.
Typical Range: 0.0 – 16.8 mm Effect: 0 mm rain sustained over days drives critical fuel dryness
Fire Weather Indices
Fine Fuel Moisture Code FFMC
Scale: 0 – 101 Higher = Increased Flammability
A numeric rating of the moisture content in surface litter and cured fine fuels (pine needles, dead leaves, small twigs). It is the primary indicator of how easily a fire can ignite and spread across surface fuels.
Typical Range: 28.6 – 92.5+ Threshold: > 80 indicates high ignition risk; > 90 is extreme
Duff Moisture Code DMC
Scale: Numeric Higher = Deeper Fuel Consumption
A numeric rating of average moisture content in loosely compacted, decomposing organic layers of moderate depth (5–10 cm). It indicates fuel consumption in medium soil layers and smoldering persistence.
Typical Range: 0.7 – 65.9+ Significance: Higher values indicate prolonged fire resistance to control
Initial Spread Index ISI
Scale: Numeric Higher = Faster Spread
A numerical rating that combines Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC) and wind speed to assess the expected rate of fire propagation immediately after ignition on level ground, excluding fuel load.
Typical Range: 0.0 – 18.5+ Role: Direct component in final FWI calculation
Classification & Geographic Location
Fire Classes Classes
Binary: 0 or 1 1 = Fire Occurrence
A categorical indicator from the training dataset representing whether a wildfire occurred under the recorded meteorological conditions: 0 = Not Fire, 1 = Fire.
Allowed Values: 0 (No Fire) or 1 (Fire)
Region Region
Binary: 0 or 1 Geographic Sector
Specifies the regional dataset location in Algeria: 0 = Bejaia region (Northeast coastal Algeria), 1 = Sidi-Bel Abbes region (Northwest interior plateau).
Allowed Values: 0 (Bejaia) or 1 (Sidi-Bel Abbes)
